Message from the Director

Dr. D.C. Sundaresh, Director, SSSIHMS, Whitefield, Bangalore

Om Sri Sai Ram, I offer myself at the Divine Lotus Feet of Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba. It is with great humility that I stand before you to offer the annual report of this temple of healing.

With the immense blessings and grace of Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba, this unique masterpiece and monument of pure love has not only stood the test of time but also grown multi-fold in the 23 years since inception.

What is so special about this hospital, which makes it one of a kind is its maxim of patient-centric service with compassion at its core? The performance of this hospital is not measured by the revenues generated or the sheer patient turnover but a larger rubric characterized by 1. Absolutely free-of-cost treatment to all those who come

to this hospital. 2. No discrimination on the basis of caste, creed, religion or

socio-economic status. 3. End-to-end treatment with comprehensive follow-up. Patient satisfaction is achieved through the interdependent efforts of expert doctors across specialties, highly trained nurses and allied-medical professionals and Sevadals or volunteers, who attend to their duties with absolute diligence, compassion and love, guided by Swami’s teaching of “Love All, Serve All”.

The hospital has an average of 850 patient visits per day. Consultations are based on prior appointments facilitated via a Tele Help Desk, managed by 200 volunteers from the Sri Sathya Sai Seva Organization, and alumni of Sri Sathya Sai Institute of Higher Learning. This has optimized process flow and yielded efficiency gains for both staff and patients. This is yet another innovative approach following Swami’s teaching of “Help Ever, Hurt Never”.

This hospital leverages state-of-the-art medical technology for providing medical care at the primary, secondary and tertiary levels absolutely free of cost. This year with the support of the Trust, several critical equipment have been upgraded.

The quality of medical care and hospital operations is measured & monitored through a dedicated mechanism of specialized committees, under the chairmanship of the Director. These include:

1. Patient Care Committee: For exclusive focus on inpatient treatment protocols.

2. Hospital Infection Control Committee: For identification, minimization, control of hospital acquired infections. 3. Clinical Meetings for reviewing department-wise medical

cases, and 4. Operational meetings for assessment of department-wise

resource requirements. These best practices account for a significantly low infection and mortality rates in the Institute, on par or better than international standards.

In addition to effective patient care, the Institute has also been instrumental in creating the next generation of doctors, nurses and technologists. More specifically, the SSSIHMS College of Nursing & Allied Health Sciences has been offering free B.Sc. courses ingrained with ‘Value Based Integral Education’ for 15 years since inception on 1-Sep-2008. The B.Sc. specializations include Anaesthesia & Operation Theatre Technology, Cardiac Technology, Perfusion Technology, Imaging Technology, Medical Laboratory Technology and Nursing. These are affiliated to the Rajiv Gandhi University of Health Sciences, Bangalore. Every academic year, 60 students are admitted across these disciplines and are provided with subsidized food and residential boarding facilities.

Recently, on 19 January 2024, the new Nursing College building has been inaugurated by Shri D.K. Shivakumar, Hon’ble Deputy Chief Minister, Govt. of Karnataka. The new facility is equipped with state-of-the-art classrooms with touchscreen interactive boards, computer lab, and training labs.

The Hospital has active post-graduate and post-doctoral programs affiliated to National Board of Examinations, offering DNB and fellowship programs, free-of-cost, across specialties.

In line with Bhagawan’s principle “Manava Seva ye Madhava Seva - Service to Man is Service to God”, the hospital has been functioning as a vast temple of healing and a temple of learning, rendering all services absolutely free of cost, with love and kindness as the fundamental ethos. This facilitates the translation of medical services into a larger dimension of Medicare.

I sincerely pray to Bhagawan to continuously inspire and bless us to do our best and be His Humble Instruments in spreading love and care to all His children.

I end with Baba’s quote, which serves as a reminder to all the staff serving at His Institutions: “Selfless Service to the needy is the highest spiritual discipline.”

Cardiac Patient Story Recipient of Divine Grace

Anand Kumar Mondal and Chandra Mondal are a loving couple, happily married in 1987 and blessed with two children. Anand worked as an accountant in a public firm, while Chandra Mondal was a housewife. A middleclass family, they lived a simple life.

In 2007, Chandra developed a fever and after a couple of days of waiting for it to subside, Anand took her to a local doctor, who prescribed her medicines. While it came down initially, it started recurring more often. Perplexed, the doctor conducted a more detailed examination, and advised an Echo test to be done. This revealed an issue with her heart for which she was referred to a cardiologist. With a lot of trepidation, they visited the local medical college hospital, where the cardiologist diagnosed Chandra with an Atrial Septal Defect (ASD) viz. a hole in the heart, which needed intervention.

The diagnosis came as a shock to the family, especially to Chandra, who was very attached to her children. Also, the lakhs of rupees quoted by the hospital for the surgery was not within their reach.

It was through a chance interaction with a family acquaintance, who received treatment for a heart ailment at Sri Sathya Sai Hospital in Bangalore, that they heard about the Temple of Healing for the first time.

In August, 2007 Anand and Chandra made their first trip to SSSIHMS. The doctors investigated her condition and informed them that Chandra would need to undergo a cardiac procedure to close the hole in the heart. After a couple of checkups, they were advised to visit the hospital in January, 2008 for admission.

It was during one of the hospital visits that they decided to visit Puttaparthi for Baba’s darshan along with their younger child. It was their first time in Prasanthi Nilayam. They were fortunate to be seated in the first line, and Baba passed them both very closely and looked straight into their eyes. Anand felt tears of joy course down his face and he experienced a lightness of the body and mind. It seemed the first Darshan dissolved his anxiety about her treatment. The peace and tranquility they experienced laid the foundation of their faith in Baba.

On 1-Jan-2008, Chandra was admitted at the hospital and she underwent the procedure on 2-Jan. Chandra narrates her experience during surgery, which is nothing short of a miracle. “I had a prayer in my heart that Baba should save me for the sake of my young children. As I was being wheeled into the operation theatre, I vividly recall Baba next to me, assuring me He would take care.”

After the procedure, Anand recalls the joy he felt when the doctors came out and conveyed the news the procedure was successful. They said that for a period of 10-12 years she would have a trouble-free health, but once she develops any further issues, she may require an open-heart surgery. Chandra’s life was uneventful after the procedure and she continued to live a happy life, with faith in Baba’s grace on the family. In 2022, 14 years after her first procedure, Chandra started complaining of tiredness, had bouts of breathlessness and experienced difficulty in climbing stairs. They visited the hospital in October 2022, when after examining her, the doctors told her to come again after one year for an open-heart surgery.

Accordingly, they planned their visit around Baba’s Birthday on 23-Nov. After visiting the Brindavan ashram on 23-Nov, they presented themselves at the Hospital, where very quickly, she was admitted for an open-heart surgery. She underwent other preliminary tests to obtain clearance for surgery. She was admitted shortly thereafter and underwent an angiogram followed by surgery on the same day. Here again, Swami chose to bless Anand in a very unique way. While Anand was sitting outside the OT complex, a Sevadal comes and hands over a beautiful picture of Baba to him. He could not believe his eyes. He was so deeply moved by Baba’s grace.

Chandra’s surgery, where she underwent a valve replacement was successful and the post-op ICU and ward stay was uneventful as well.

Narrating his experiences through his years of visiting SSSIHMS, Anand says, “I have learnt so many beautiful life lessons visiting the hospital about how Seva is to be carried out and how to interact with other people. I have learnt to talk gently and constantly recall Baba’s teaching of “Love All, Serve All, Help Ever Hurt Never” and try and follow it all my life. We owe our lives to Bhagawan.

From her side, Chandra Mondal says, “I have deep faith in Baba, and through His instruments, be they the doctors, nurses or the Sevadal. I am immensely grateful for all the love and support we have received at the Hospital, and are ever indebted to Baba”.

Cardiac Patient Story · page 7

Grace of God

Kalyani, a 14-year girl hailing from West Bengal, presented to our hospital with bluish discoloration of nails and lips and breathlessness on doing anything more than a household activity.

The Cardiology team evaluated her and referred her to our department for surgery as she had an extremely complex congenital lesion called CCTGA-VSD-PS with situs inversus and mesocardia. In layman’s terms, the organs supposed to be on the right are on the left and vice versa, including structures of the heart. Also, the heart instead of being tilted to the left, was in the centre. Atrial chambers, which receive blood from the body and lungs (deoxygenated and oxygenated respectively) were connected to the wrong ventricles (the pumping chambers), which was opposite of the normal. Along with this, there was a big hole in the heart, between these pumping chambers.

To add to this complexity, the big blood vessels which supply oxygenated blood to the body and deoxygenated blood to the lung were taking off from ventricles in a fashion opposite to the normal connection and the blood vessel going to the lung was very small near the opening, which was the reason for the bluish discoloration.

After thorough examination and reviewing the ECHO and CT scan, we planned a procedure called Senning-Rastelli (Double switch operation) to address Mother Nature’s play of switching connections at two levels.

On the day of surgery, everything was set. After opening the chest, we were amazed to see the complexity of the heart. The surgery went on smoothly and repair came out well. Due to the longer duration of the surgery, the chest was kept open for that night and was closed on the next day. She was taken off the ventilator on the next day and was shifted to the ward subsequently. She is still dependent on the pacemaker to support her heart rate, as the heart rate is around 50, which is little less for her present condition. It is a huge accomplishment to survive such a complex congenital heart lesion for 14 years and recover from such a complex surgery in a short duration. To address the heart rate, a pacemaker was implanted and she was discharged, fit to go out and live a fruitful life.

Dr. Ikshu Dhanva Jr. Consultant, Dept. of CTVS

Sevadal Story · page 8

Healing Grace

Sairam. On 27-Sep-23 my wife and I proceeded to Majestic Railway Station for our pilgrimage to Shirdi & Pandaripur.

We boarded the train in time. Just 5-10 minutes before the departure, I experienced giddiness and collapsed on the floor. No one was around me at that time. By Swami’s love and blessings, I got up on my own. As I landed on the floor, I heard the crack of a bone. Swami helped me realize my left side collar bone was fractured.

Upon seeing me get up, my wife, who returned to the compartment, gave me turmeric powder blessed by Swami and applied vibuthi on the left shoulder. Through this, our Swami has absorbed the pain caused by the fracture. To the surprise of one and all I felt not a pinch of pain at all till this minute.

Before we could decide further course of action, the TTE arrived on the scene. He arranged an ambulance & wheel chair at the next station, Yelahanka, on the outskirts of Bangalore city.

Swami, gave me the intuition it would not be wise to continue the pilgrimage. Hence, we broke our journey and reached Swami’s Temple of Healing at Whitefield by 10.30 pm.

Upon reaching the hospital, all necessary tests including an X-ray were done. The Ortho doctor visited me, and confirmed the collar bone fracture. He added that the fracture did not need surgery and that a sling and a fourweek rest should be adequate.

To find out the cause for sudden giddiness, ENT, Neurology and Cardiology departments carried out all necessary tests including CT scan, lab tests, ECG, ECHO & holter study for 48 hours. I was relieved to find that all health parameters were within normal range, thanks to Swami’s grace! It is assumed the time gap between lunch & dinner on that day (about 6 hours) would have caused low sugar, leading to giddiness and the fall.

I consider this accident has been a blessing in disguise, and that Swami saved us from a more serious calamity by having us experience this minor injury, and for which He has taken care of me very well through His instruments in His Temple of Healing!

We were in for another blessing, when I visited the Ortho doctor in October, to get the clavicle brace adjusted, as it was rubbing against the skin and causing some irritation. When the doctor opened the brace to adjust it, he found a small picture of the Hiranyagarba lingam inside the brace. He inquired why we put this picture inside the brace, and we reported that we did not place the picture. Swami seemed to have placed this picture at the site of fracture, to hasten the healing!

I am back to serving in the Temple of Healing from Nov-2023. We are immensely grateful to Swami for his profuse shower of Healing Grace on me and my family.

Staff Story · page 9

Healing Grace

While SSSIHMS-Whitefield was inaugurated on 19-Jan-2001 with cardiac and neuro specialties, one of the lesser known facts is that perhaps the first emergency surgery done in the hospital was a plastic surgery done well before its inauguration.

It was during September 2000, when the inauguration was still a few months away. The medical staff employed were very few and there were mostly non-medical / engineering staff, working in preparation for the inauguration.

It was a relaxed Sunday morning, when I received a call from the medical administrator, requesting me to come to the hospital urgently. When I rushed to the hospital, I was briefed that an engineering staff had three of his fingers cut off in a freak accident. The three fingers were safely stored in a bottle and the search was on for a plastic surgeon, who could help fix these back. We contacted the honorary plastic surgeon, who was serving in the Sri Sathya Sai General Hospital, Whitefield, but he was not reachable.

It was post lunch by the time we could reach him, and he readily agreed to come carry out the surgery. Though we had the surgeon, we did not have the instruments! Since we were a cardiac and neuro centre, we did not have the specific instruments needed to carry out this procedure. With constant chanting of ‘Sai Ram’, we selected available instruments best suited for the purpose and proceeded with the surgery. The theatre was not even ready and there was no anaesthetist available. The surgeon himself anesthetized the patient and carried out the surgery.

With the grace of Bhagawan, the surgery was a success! We needed post-operative dressing for the site of surgery, which we did not have in stock. The medical administrator and I scoured the local pharmacies and finally found the required dressing 5 km away in a newly-opened pharmacy! The pharmacist offered the dressings for free, apparently as they were samples provided by a company, as part of their product promotion.

When I retired that night, I had a dream of Swami in the early morning hours, enquiring whether all the dressings were consumed and whether we needed more for the days ahead. I woke up with tears of joy flowing from my eyes, as that was my first opportunity of communication with Swami after surrendering at His Divine Lotus feet in 1985, when I was in the middle east.

This episode firmly planted in my mind that it is the Healing Grace of Swami, that pervades this Temple of Healing, and we are truly fortunate to be part of His healthcare mission, delivering free healthcare to the most deserving.

It is heartening to note that the staff member, whose fingers were mended, is still serving at the Institute.

Cover Story · pages 10–13

Nursing College

Let’s go back in time 33 years. It is 14-Jul-1992, Guru Poornima day, almost eight months after SSSIHMS Prasanthigram was inaugurated on 22-Nov-1991. Bhagawan started His discourse speaking of how Indian doctors were going abroad, due to inadequate recognition and encouragement. He then says, “Realizing we should make use of their services here itself, Swami has inspired many highly eminent doctors to serve in our hospital”.

He then follows this with, “When we have such highly capable and dedicated doctors, Swami willed that there should be sufficient numbers of trained nursing personnel and therefore training facilities for nursing personnel will be established. It is easy for doctors to perform operations. The real difficulty is in taking care of the patients after the operations. It is easy to slice vegetables.

But, to prepare a tasty dish out of that is very difficult. This task of “cooking” is done by the nurses. If there is no good nursing, even the operation may be a failure.”

This is when He declared His Sankalpa for starting a training facility to meet the nursing needs of “ever-growing number of patients seeking relief”.

Excerpts from His discourse:

“…in order to train a good band of nursing personnel, training facilities are being provided. All those who are pure-hearted and filled with a spirit of service, may approach Dr. Safaya (Director-SSSIHMS Prasanthigram) for undergoing training to serve in the hospital. They need not incur any expense. The entire training is free. Elsewhere, if people want to undergo such training, they have to pay all kinds of fees. Here nothing will be charged. Everything will be provided free. Only, we want persons with pure hearts to join the course.”

Today we have quite a large number of people working in our hospital. But, there is more work to be done because of the ever-growing number of patients seeking relief. Hence, we need many more nursing personnel. We are asking for nurses not for Swami’s sake but for the sake of the nation.”

Bhagawan always said that in the sacred land of Bharath, when anyone thinks a good thought, the Universe rushes in to fulfill it. In this case, while He could have easily willed a Nursing College into existence, He invested love, time and energy into this project, which resulted in the starting of the Nursing College at SSSIHMS-Whitefield on 1-Sep-2008 and culminating in the inauguration of the new Nursing College Block on 19-Jan-2024 fifteen years later.

Following are a few features of the new Nursing College Block, inaugurated by the Honourable Deputy CM of Karnataka, Sri D.K. Shivakumar:

  • Area: 23,200 sft
  • Classrooms: 4 nos, with 60 seating capacity •Labs: 6 nos. 1. Pre-clinical Science Lab 2. Child Health Nursing Lab 3. Obstetrics & Gynecology Nursing Lab 4. Community Health Nursing & Nutrition Lab 5. Computer Lab 6. Nursing Foundation, including Adult Health Nursing & Advance Nursing Lab

After the interview process, their applications were blessed by Bhagawan and Smt. Manjuri was appointed as the Principal for the Nursing College, a post she continues to hold now for 15 years.

Soon after the College started functioning, Bhagawan sent a few elders to start the Education in Human Values (EHV) program in the Nursing College, making it perhaps the only healthcare Institution, which has a value-based education and self-development curriculum, along with the clinical curriculum. The students are also trained in singing bhajans with devotion and Vedam chanting.

Though the college is affiliated to the RGUHS, Bhagawan allowed the Nursing & AHS students to participate in the Annual Sports & Cultural Meet of the Sri Sathya Sai Institute of Higher Learning, held on 11-Jan of every year.

After their contractual work period of 3 years after the program, the Nursing and AHS students are all very well placed, with the majority of them serving in India, and a good percentage of them serving in other countries in the middle-east, UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, and Singapore.

The students have earned a good name for themselves and their Alma mater, even during their clinical postings. One of the senior administrators of a psychiatric hospital remarked how he looks forward to receiving nursing students from the Institute as part of their clinical posting. “Through their love and affection towards the patients, and soulful bhajans, they transform the mood of the whole Institution during their period of stay. Their good nature, smiling demeanor, professional expertise, inquiring minds speak volumes about the value-based education they received at SSSIHMS College of Nursing,” he mentioned.

We pray to Bhagawan to bless the Nursing College, and the staff with the strength, courage and will to provide value-based education to the nursing students, who will serve the Nation to the best of their abilities.

In Conversation · pages 14–15

Ms. Sheela Nayak

Mano Hriday (MH): Sai Ram Ms. Nayak. Could you please share how you came to Swami?

Sheela Nayak (SN): Sai Ram. I first saw Swami in 1972, when I was 31 years old. I had gone to India, and my sisters, who were already visiting Swami, wanted me to see Him. I did not know anything about Swami at the time. During the interview He blessed me with, He asked me, “What do you want?” and my mind went blank. I mentioned I wanted to return to India after my studies and He said, “No, stay where you are!” And He showed His hand with five fingers and said, “In five years, East will be like West and West will be like East. Stay there.” That was my first conversation with Baba.

Since then, I completed my Bachelors and Masters in early childhood education and have been in the US ever since.

MH: Could you narrate any experiences from your first few visits to Prasanthi Nilayam?

SN: Sure. While working with young children, my health condition required I had to take a flu shot every year, which I did not like doing. However, after the first darshan of Swami, I realized my immunity got a boost that I never had to take the shot again.

I am an asthmatic and prone to frequent attacks. During my second visit to Prasanthi in 1976, I stood outside the mandir, as I felt the onset of an attack and did not want to disturb the other devotees. I was filming Swami with a video camera and, believe it or not, Swami came outside and stood right in front of me and raised His hand in blessing. It’s entirely His Grace that I did not have an asthma attack since then.

MH: And how did your involvement in the Sai centres in the US begin?

SN: My parents were instrumental in this. During their visit to the US, they wanted to visit the local Sai centre, which happened to be the first centre in the US. I was reluctant to enter the centre, but circumstances worked out such a way that I met the Centre head, who hailed from Karnataka. He struck up a conversation with me and enquired about my husband, Ramesh, who was working in Kuwait at the time. He (the Centre head) insisted that we should stay together and suggested I bring Ramesh’s CV to him. Within a few weeks of having done that, Ramesh, who did his Ph.D in Cell Biology, miraculously received an interview call from the National Institute of Health, USA. He took up the job, and served the NIH for over 30 years. This is how we, as a couple, started our family in the US.

By now, Swami’s presence had suffused our lives and I have been witness to the openings of the first few Sai centres in the US.

MH: Please tell us about the first assignment Swami gave you. SN: In 1981, the Centre head wanted me to organize a trip to Puttaparthi for Swami’s darshan. I did all the necessary background work to facilitate the trip. Race against Time

Pappu Kanp, or Pappu as we called him affectionately, came to us in a really bad physical and mental state.

He was born to a hardworking gentleman of a humble background, earning his living as a truck driver. He is the youngest of the three children. He had a lot of responsibility on his tiny shoulders with only months away from the board exams.

A playful but studious child, he described how he first realized the problem, when he was unable to kick the football. Later this affected his ability to grip a pen. He gradually started losing power in his legs and hands over the period of three years.

The subtle signs of weakness was mistaken by the local physicians for a muscle spasm. His confidence started waning day by day, and it reached a time in June, 2023 that he was confined to bed and needed his parents help for basic activities like food or wearing clothes. Local doctors were consulted upon repeatedly and this time he was diagnosed with proximal myopathy, a condition that affects skeletal muscles that attach to and moves our bones.

His general condition worsened, the body became frail with every passing day and he started having difficulty breathing. He started gasping for air and that rang an alarm in the scared household. He was taken to a nearby neurologist, who ordered an MRI of the spine. There was a delay in getting it done due to financial constraints.

Once done, the MRI revealed a tumor inside the spinal cord, controlling the motor, respiratory and bladder functions; extending an enormous length of 60 cm, right from brainstem in the head to upper back region. The fear struck parents, with no funds to get even the MRI scan, were suddenly required to pay Rs. 15 lakh for surgery. Each passing moment was hell and their hopes became bleaker by the passing day. One day, a relative informed them about the Sri Sathya Sai Hospital, offering advanced neurosurgery services for free.

He came with almost no power in the upper limbs and minimal movements against the gravity in the lower limbs. He required multiple pauses for completing simple sentences because of air hunger. He was admitted to the intensive care unit. The preoperative blood parameters and respiratory function were optimized. He was taken up for the excision of the lesion. A laminoplasty was done using the state-of-the-art bone CUSA machine, which means the posterior part of spine had to be cut to reach the tumour with minimal bone loss and reattached back after tumour excision. With improved neurophysiological monitoring techniques and vast experience of Dr Sumit Thakar, consultant neurosurgeon, in treating these kinds of lesions, which arise directly in the center of the spinal cord, requiring an incision over the posterior part of spinal cord, the tumour was removed totally from its cranial to caudal extent.

Post-operatively, he could not be weaned off the ventilator and had to undergo a tracheostomy for airway protection after surgery. The fight against, time which started three years back was finally over, but now the fight was much bigger for Pappu - the fight for functionality. He was started on rigorous physiotherapy and after a difficult period of two months, he was weaned off the ventilator.

The struggle was not over yet; the trial to remove the tracheostomy tube failed multiple times. To avoid any complications arising from the same at home or during travel he was discharged with the tracheostomy tube. He regained almost 50-60% power in his both upper and lower limbs and was taking feeds orally. His willpower to fight back the disease made him do the impossible, by not only avoiding the mortality associated with such tumors but also by regaining adequate neurological capability. They had exhausted all the savings and had no money for the suction apparatus or the wheelchair or the train tickets. The benevolence of the staff was evident again and through anonymous donations of good Samaritans, everything was arranged.

The fight wasn’t over yet and the train journey was the most treacherous one. The secretions can block the tube and choke the airway without suctioning. The worst fears came true when the suction couldn’t be connected to any of the plug points and Pappu gasped for air. Without any immediate medical assistance, his father did something which is nothing short of a miracle and demonstrated the immense love of a father towards his son. He fixed his mouth to Pappu’s mouth and sucked out the secretions, clearing the choking mucous plugs away and allowing him to breathe, until the emergency medical service was available. At the time of writing this article Pappu is doing well and spoke over the phone clearly and positively. He is due for a review a month later for removal of the tracheostomy tube. He not only fought against the time lost in getting the right treatment, but also against the morbidity that is associated with such intramedullary tumors.

It was all possible due to the hardworking nursing staff who toiled day and night, the physiotherapy staff for respiratory and motor rehabilitation and lastly the willpower of Pappu and his parents to fight back against the disease. Dr. Mohammed Khizar DNB Trainee, Dept. of Neurosurgery

Neuro Patient Story · page 29

Never Give up

Genda Lal Soni is a 43-year-old shopkeeper from Bihar, who visited our screening OPD on 30-Jun-22 with a 3-year history of blurring of vision. He was evaluated for the same at a local centre and found to have a tumour in the pituitary gland, which had grown significantly, causing compression of the optic chiasma, causing visual blurring.

We evaluated him for any disturbances in the hormonal balance, which is common in these tumours. The ophthalmologist examined him in detail for his visual status and concluded to have peripheral field restriction of the vision, which is characteristic of tumours in this region. He underwent an endoscopic trans-nasal trans-sphenoidal approach and excision under general anaesthesia on 17-Jun-22. In this, we reach the pituitary gland through the nose and take out the tumour. Surgery was uneventful and he was in the ICU. The patient did well post-operatively and he was shifted to the ward.

On the second post-operative day, Genda Lal complained that he was not seeing anything in his right eye. The MRI found an operative cavity blood clot was compressing his right-sided optic nerve. He was rushed to the operative room on Sunday morning and an emergency evacuation of the hematoma was done. He regained his right eye vision to the same level as the preoperative state.

He was doing well in the ward, when suddenly he started having a watery leak from the nose – CSF rhinorrhoea. Insertion of a lumbar drain to divert CSF was attempted, but the drain could not be inserted in the ICU because of his unusual anatomical variation. He was taken to the OT and a lumbar drain was inserted by removing part of the bone of lumbar vertebra at L3 level.

This did help transiently, but the leak recurred. He was taken up again for endoscopic repair of the leak followed by the peritoneal shunt – a thin tube that drains CSF from lumbar subarachnoid space into the abdominal cavity. He was doing well transiently, then again leakage recurred from the nose.

We decided to seal the leak from the inside portion of the skull itself and he underwent Bifrontal craniotomy and intradural repair of a defect in Sella with vascularised pericranium under GA on 9-Jul-22.

At last, his CSF leak stopped and a much larger problem was waiting for giving him more trouble. After a period of doing well, he became dull and the sensorium went down. He started having a fever, CT brain showed enlarged ventricles with hydrocephalus. Extra ventricular drain (EVD) was inserted and CSF showed infection and appropriate antibiotic were started both intravenous and intrathecal route.

EVD tube kept on blocking and it was replaced once. He was on antibiotics, as he fought a deadly condition known as ventriculitis. Our attempts and timely interventions every time he had an issue saved his vision and life multiple times. During all these, he had a turbulent course concerning his sodium levels. With the background of pituitary tumour, infection and hydrocephalus, his electrolyte imbalance was challenging to manage but it was done successfully. Once his infection was confirmed to be negative, we did a permanent ventriculoperitoneal shunt and the patient was discharged successfully to be followed up with the endocrinologist for his hormonal imbalance.

The entire team of neurosurgeons put in their best effort every time to get him out of the turbulence he faced. In spite of three months of struggle, multiple surgeries and distressed family members, it was rewarding to see that at the end, Genda Lal Soni is healthy and is coming for his regular follow-ups.

Dr. Prashanth Alle DNB Trainee, Dept. of Neurosurgery
