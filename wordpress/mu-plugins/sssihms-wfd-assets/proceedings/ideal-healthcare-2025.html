Welcome Address

Dr. Sundaresh DC, Director, SSSIHMS, Whitefield

Read transcript 890 words

I offer my humble salutations at the Divine Lotus Feet of our dear Lord, Bhagwan Sri Sathya Sai Baba. Respected trustees of the Sri Sathya Sai Central Trust, invited guests, revered elders, dear Sai brothers and sisters, it is my privilege to welcome you to this global conference on the Sathya Sai Ideal Health Care Mission. I am happy to note that the response from doctors all over India and from overseas has been overwhelming. We have over 800 registered delegates. I warmly welcome all who have joined us.

This conference is being organized to rededicate ourselves to the Sathya Sai Health Care Mission and as part of the centenary celebrations of Bhagwan Baba's advent. The fact that this health care model has flourished for seven decades now proves its robustness and the overflowing grace of Bhagwan. The work being done here is reflected in the faces of millions who have benefited. Our goal is to spread this message of love through health care delivery, to encourage more people to participate in this mission, and to emulate it in their own environments.

The Sri Sathya Sai General Hospital at Prashantinilayam, established at Mother Eshwaramba's request, began in 1956 as a small hospital with eight beds. Today, it has grown to a 120-bed multi-speciality hospital, serving more than 500,000 people within a 12-kilometre radius, across over 300 villages. Following this, the Sathya Sai General Hospital at Whitefield was established as a mother and child hospital to meet the needs of the local population. At that time, Whitefield was outside Bangalore city limits. The General Hospital, now located at the premises of the Sri Sathya Sai Institute of Higher Medical Sciences at Whitefield, has served countless patients through the years. Today, the hospital has an outpatient strength of over 500 per day and performs 12 to 15 major surgeries daily in its four operating rooms.

Importantly, all services are free, yet there is no compromise in the quality of care, manpower, or equipment. Volunteer doctors of all specialties come from across India and the world to serve patients here. This general specialty hospital covers all fields: general surgery, medicine, orthopedics, ENT, ophthalmology, urology, and obstetrics and gynecology. The hallmark since inception has been the number of volunteer doctors who contribute regularly and on a scheduled basis, ensuring patients receive expert help when they need it.

During the 65th birthday celebrations in 1990, Bhagwan Baba announced the establishment of a super specialty hospital for cardiac patients at Puttaparthi—a surprise for many, given the rural setting. Yet, a palatial building of 350,000 square feet was ready within a year, and the first operation took place in 1991. The hospital was inaugurated on November 22, 1991. This became the third unit of the Sathyasai Ideal Health Care Mission, gradually expanding to include specialties such as urology, ophthalmology, orthopedics, plastic surgery, and gastroenterology.

A similar announcement led to the Super Speciality Hospital at Whitefield in 2000. A year later, in 2001, a new building of almost 325,000 square feet was constructed—primarily for cardiac and neurosciences. Both specialties were led by senior doctors and equipped with state-of-the-art equipment for complex surgeries. The hospital boasts 12 fully equipped operating rooms and the latest diagnostic technology.

Not satisfied with merely stationary hospitals, Baba initiated the Mobile Hospital in 2006, providing holistic health care to over 500,000 people in 300 villages around Puttaparthi. This unique model will be further discussed over the next two days. The unique selling proposition of all these hospitals is what we call the ideal health care mission of Baba: universal, free, comprehensive, compassionate, and preventive. All of this is done with the best technology and equipment, with no compromise on quality.

Our outpatient department is entirely paperless and fully digital, reducing our carbon footprint. The high-quality, free services attract patients from all over the country, resulting in large numbers, but we have managed this effectively through a robust appointment system—patients are seen on the same day as their appointment, without having to wait outside. Volunteerism is the hallmark of the mission, with doctors and healthcare professionals supported by Sevadars from the Sathya Sai organization, who perform all kinds of tasks to support service delivery.

The Sathya Sai Central Trust provides constant support, with costs exceeding 1,000 crores—100 crores each year—and likely more in the future. The funds come from unsolicited donations from Baba's devotees, with hospital expenditures funded through interest generated on a corpus fund. This model is possible, sustainable, and replicable, as proven by its existence for over 70 years. Bhagwan Baba's infinite love for humanity and desire to alleviate suffering is at the heart of this mission. Anyone inspired by this feeling can continue the work—there is no dearth of money, only a need for love for fellow human beings.

We now have memoranda of understanding with two leading universities: Tokyo Science University in Japan and the University of Chicago Medicine Department in the United States. This collaboration will facilitate exchange of skills and research across these institutions. In the coming two days at the Sathya Sai Heera Convention Center, we will deliberate on these aspects in greater detail. I pray to Bhagwan to bless us all, and I urge medical professionals to join this voyage under the direct guidance of Bhagwan Sathya Sai Baba and make their lives truly meaningful. Jai Sairam.

Primary Healthcare “The Lift -Off”

Read transcript 1,004 words

My humble salutations at the Lotus Feet of Bhagwan Baba, esteemed trustees, respected elders, and my dear colleagues. We are all gathered here today to commemorate 100 years of the Sai Avatar and 70 years of selfless medical service to humanity. It is my pleasant duty to guide you through the genesis and remarkable journey of this medical mission, which began with the foundation of the Sri Sathya Sai General Hospital in 1954.

The hospital’s inception was rooted in the fulfillment of a promise made by Bhagwan Baba to Mother Eshwaramma—to establish a small hospital in the village of Puttaparthi. In the 1950s, Puttaparthi was a rustic and remote hamlet, yet the Avatar chose this humble setting to demonstrate two core principles of Sanatana Dharma: that the mother and motherland are holier than heaven itself, and that education (Vidya) and healthcare (Vaidya) should not be commercialized.

Allow me to guide you through a pictorial journey of the General Hospital’s milestones. The foundation was laid by the Divine hands on November 23rd, 1954. The original one-storey building was constructed entirely by devoted volunteers. Dr. Sitaramaiah Garu was the first Medical Superintendent, and this early period was so dear to Bhagwan that he personally took photographs of the hospital staff.

On October 4th, 1956, Swami inaugurated the hospital. Over the years, the hospital grew organically, evolving alongside the organization. In the 1960s, mobile medical services were launched, reaching nearby villages. By the 1970s, as more people flocked to Puttaparthi, hospital doctors began holding medical camps in the Poornachandra Auditorium—a practice that continues today in both India and abroad through the Sri Sathya Sai Organizations.

In 1984, the main hospital building, which now houses the operation theatre complex and maternity wing, was constructed. In 1990, the extension block and the Super Specialty Hospital with the NICU were established. The latest expansion in 2015 now houses the Emergency and General Medicine departments.

What began as an 8-bed hospital has grown into a 120-bed facility. The number of patients served is a testament to the hospital’s impact: we see nearly 1,000 patients daily in the OPD—amounting to around 300,000 visits annually—and admit approximately 16,000 patients each year.

Let me provide an overview of the hospital’s extensive services. The Obstetrics and Gynecology Department is truly the bedrock of this institution—it would not be an exaggeration to say that nearly every person born in the last 50 years in Puttaparthi and the surrounding villages was delivered in our labor rooms. Annually, we record 1,400 deliveries (about four per day) and 1,000 pediatric patient visits. Our Level 2 B NICU supports this care, and the Pediatrics Department sees 120 children daily. We provide end-to-end care for type 1 diabetes, including insulin, glucometers, lancets, and sick day management.

The Department of General Medicine is the backbone of the hospital, treating nearly 500 patients every day. We manage chronic diseases—such as diabetes, hypertension, COPD, bronchial asthma, and coronary artery disease—providing comprehensive care from cradle to grave. The Emergency Department operates 24 hours a day and sees about 70 patients daily, comparable to some smaller medical college hospitals. We are a referral center for snake bites in the district, managing 300–400 cases per year and administering more than 3,000 vials of anti-snake venom. Notably, we are the only facility in the region with immunoglobulin for rabies.

Support services include dietary, laboratory, and pharmacy. The Dental Department serves 22,000 patients annually and provides orthodontic treatments for children associated with the Sri Sathya Sai organizations. We also offer General Surgery, Anesthesia, Ophthalmology, ENT, Audiology, and several ancillary specialties. The Yoga and Lifestyle Disease Clinic focuses on health education and preventive care.

Our hospital is accredited for 2 DNB Family Medicine seats, a program begun in 2008 with a 100% pass rate. Our students attest to receiving excellent clinical training, enriched by both inpatient and outpatient care, all within a spiritual atmosphere that fosters competent, confident, and compassionate doctors.

The financial impact of the hospital is immense. The market value of our services exceeds ₹24,00,00,000—a conservative estimate that only covers some expenses. This has substantially reduced out-of-pocket healthcare costs for the community, saving countless families from debt and poverty, improving financial stability, and leading to better health outcomes.

A recent survey by the students of Sri Sathya Sai Institute of Higher Learning assessed patient satisfaction across categories such as accessibility, convenience, time with the doctor, interpersonal manner, and technical quality. The hospital scored between 4.7 and 4.9 out of 5 in every category. Importantly, the intangible spiritual impact is profound: many patients attend not only because services are free or convenient but because of the hospital’s reputation and their faith in Bhagwan Baba. As Swami has said, patients who come to this aarogya nilaya leave restored in both health and spirit, equipped with faith to guard them against future illness.

Some unique aspects of the hospital include its distinction as the very first hospital under the direct guidance of the Avatar. We enjoyed close proximity to Bhagwan Baba, who at times personally consulted patients and dispensed medicine. Our dedicated doctors and nurses consider Seva not just an act of service but a way of life; many have served for decades, quietly and without remuneration, motivated by devotion and love. The work environment here is truly unique—free of competition and comparison, and, as our students say, a place with zero toxicity.

Our visiting doctors, renowned internationally, come from diverse specialties such as dermatology, ophthalmology, endocrinology, and psychiatry, enriching patient care and sharing their expertise. The Seva Dals—the silent army of Swami—come every fortnight from all over India, handling the hospital’s entire housekeeping needs.

In conclusion, the impact of Seva on those who serve here is even greater than the service itself. For us, this workplace is a sanctuary—a space where maladies born of Maya are cured by the Divine Doctor, and where spirituality and medicine meet. On behalf of the entire General Hospital family, I express deep gratitude to the Lord for the opportunity to serve in His hospital.
