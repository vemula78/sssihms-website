Editorial

Dear readers,

Each time we bring out an issue of Manohriday, we ask ourselves this question -“What is it that you, our readers, would like to know?” Great work happens at Swami’s hospital in a quiet, selfless way. Swami believed that our greatest advertisements are the smiles of poor patients that are healed, rather than headline news in national media. With this in mind, we bring to you a transcript of a talk made by Dr A.S. Hegde, our erstwhile Director, during the hospital’s anniversary celebrations, recounting precious advice given by our founder and his vision of the road ahead

Its five years since Swami’s last visit to the hospital in the physical form. We go back in time to relive the memories from his previous visits to the Hospital and his interactions with the staff and patients.

Swami continues to inspire us in our pursuit of excellence, as can be seen by the numerous conferences conducted at the hospital, inviting faculty from around the country to participate. We are constantly pushing the bar on our services to patients, be it ensuring strict compliance to hand hygiene, introducing programs in basic life support (BLS) and acute cardiac life support (ACLS), or strengthening our nursing training program. Our doctors are constantly “sharpening their saw”, with international training and conference visits, and we continue to excel academically, as can be seen by a spurt in national awards bestowed on our staff.

The hospital continues to be a great place beyond work too, with families of staff bonding over aeroshows, music programs and art sessions. We have also opened our doors to those that are devoted to service, by engaging software professionals in Bangalore to experience the joy of volunteering at the hospital. We believe that his message of love should go around, and we can’t be selfish about what we have, can we?

– The Editors

Anesthesia Conference

The SSSIHMS Anesthesia Conference (SANCON) 2013 on Anesthesia Machine & Monitoring Equipments was held on 29-30 March. The focus of SANCON was towards integrating textbook with equipment use to suit day-to-day OT Anesthesia practices. Anesthesia equipment and instrumentation is currently a neglected area in postgraduate medical teaching in India. SANCON provided an opportunity to address this gap, by facilitating delegate interaction with well renowned faculty using didactic sessions & workstations.

The two day conference had 18 oral and 8 workstation-based presentations held by leading professionals in Anesthesia in Bangalore & across India. Over 180 medical practitioners from across India were exposed to the latest Anaethesia equipment that is currently available.

A separate session during the conference focused on post graduate medical students facing the Diplomate of National Board (DNB) Practical exams in Anesthesia. Dr. Baljit Singh, one of the leading anesthesiologists in the country, gave a number of tips to face the rigorous exam with confidence.

Dr. K. Muralidhar, Professor, Anesthesia, Narayana Hrudayalaya, spoke on Cardiovascular Monitoring during sedation.

Dr. Kolli Chalam, HOD of Anesthesiology, SSSIHMS-WFD, elaborated on technological advancements in equipment related to pediatric anesthetic care.

Dr. Sushant Thapa GE Healthcare provided insights on how adequacy of Anesthesia can be measured in high risk patients through advanced monitoring equipment.

Dr. Thanga Prabhu, Clinical Director GE Healthcare spoke on Anesthesia Data Management Systems & e Charting solutions for critical care patients.

Dr. Pankaj Kundra of JIPMER delved on the latest techniques in Anesthesia delivery systems and touched on how the technology evolved over the years.

There was a separate session on Monitored Anesthesia Care, comprising five lectures focused on different monitoring techniques to cover aspects related to Airway, Neuromuscular, Neurological, and Cardiovascular factors.

Considering the increasing usage of ultrasound in Anesthesia, a separate session of four lectures concentrated on how ultrasound assists in optimization of patient management within the setting of Anesthesia. Ultrasound-guidance in regional Anesthesia, vascular access & hemodynamic monitoring were delved in depth by Dr. Sanjaya Viswamitra of SSSIHMS & Dr. Manimala Rao of Yashodha Hosiptal, Hyderabad.

The second day witnessed a rewarding panel discussion on Patent Safety & Quality, where faculty provided real world experience on tools & techniques they currently use to sustain high levels of patient safety during Anesthesia procedures.

On both days, the workshops spanned four topics: Anesthesia Machines, Monitoring, Ultrasound, eCharting & data management. GE Healthcare brought in their engineers to demonstrate the intricacies of the latest equipment in Anesthesia Monitoring and Ultrasound.

The participants pointed out that seminars on the usage of OT Anesthesia equipment are not held anywhere in Bangalore and rated the SANCON conference at par with the other two forums on this subject - RACE & ARC (held by Sri Ramachandra University & Tata Memorial Hospital).

SANCON aims to be an annual forum where experts in Anesthesia can share their experiences with young medical professionals and inspire them to be responsible anesthesiologists.

Patient Story

The voice on the phone seemed deeply concerned. “Sister, how is Naveen?” It was a patient’s brother calling all from far-away Kolkata. The patient had received physiotherapy after undergoing neurosurgery and B. S. Naveen Kumar’s counsel and support during the time was deeply cherished. During the 12 years spent at SSSIHMS, he had offered physiotherapy to innumerable patients. In a freak incident, he was now a patient himself, and all who had heard about his condition were praying for him.

It all happened on a November night. Driving back to Bangalore in the wee hours rushing to get back to work at the hospital, the unexpected brought his life to standstill. “I remember waking up to find out that we were in a crushed car,” recalls his wife Vishalakshi. “Naveen was groaning beside me, his face disfigured and smeared with blood”. In spite of other hospitals being on the way, she decided to go with her faith and chose to bring him to the Whitefield hospital. The journey took a few hours and all along the way the she had to constantly encourage Naveen to stay awake and stay strong. Faith in Swami was the only driving force & support during the next 45 days. Naveen’s injuries were serious, requiring Neurosurgical and Maxillofacial surgeries to correct the displaced bones of his face. The doctors told Vishalakshi that there might be some deficits post surgery, and he may not be able to see in one eye. But Vialakshi knew that Swami who had brought them along this far, would not abandon them that easily.

A popular saying speaks of blessings that descend in reply to the prayers that ascend. Every evening Vishalakshi would sit before Swami’s altar at the hospital’s central dome with a single prayer. “Both my husband and I decided to serve all these years. Please allow us to continue to serve You. This is a time we need You”. The entire hospital had turned into an extended family and everyone had a prayer in their heart. Even during his recovery his optimism was infectious. When anyone would mention to him that they want him back at work, he would reply with grit and determination, “Tell them (patients) that I am coming. I am waiting to get back to work.”

Naveen is back at work today, offering instruction and counsel to patients that come to him each day for rehabilitation therapy. He can see fairly well now, but for a minor deficit in one eye. His optimism and cheerfulness continue to infect all those who work with him. “I’ve been a patient. I know how it feels,” he says each time he greets these folk with a smile. Many marvel at his recovery, saying that he is a testimony to the fact that miracles happen. He only smiles, and shares a priceless moment from the nightmare he has just emerged from. “When I was being carried away from the car after the accident, I vividly recall seeing Swami in a white robe before me,” he says. “I was in tremendous pain, and, in that state, asked Swami to save my life only if I was destined to return to work, strong and independent.” One thing is certain. When you’ve served the lord with all your heart, He won’t let you go that easily.
