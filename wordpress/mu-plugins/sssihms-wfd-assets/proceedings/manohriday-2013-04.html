Editorial

Dear readers,

Each time we bring out an issue of Manohriday, we ask ourselves the question, “What is it that you, our readers, would like to know?” Great work happens at Swami’s hospital in a quiet, selfless way. Swami believed that our greatest advertisements are the smiles of poor patients that are healed, rather than headline news in the national media. With this in mind, we bring you a talk by Dr. A.S. Hegde, our erstwhile Director, during the hospital’s anniversary celebrations; recounting the precious advice of our founder and His vision of the road ahead.

It’s five years since Swami’s last visit to the hospital. We go back in time to relive the memories from His past visits to the hospital and His interactions with the staff and patients.

Swami continues to inspire us in our pursuit of excellence, as seen by the numerous conferences conducted at the hospital with participation from around the country. We are constantly pushing the bar on our services to patients, be it ensuring strict compliance with hand hygiene, introducing programs in basic life support (BLS) and Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS), or strengthening our nursing training program. Our doctors are constantly “sharpening their saws”, with international training and conferences, and we continue to excel academically, as evident by the spurt in national awards bestowed on our staff.

The hospital continues to be a great place beyond work too; with families of staff bonding over aeroshows, music programs and art sessions. We have also opened our doors to those devoted to service, engaging Bangalore’s software professionals in the joy of volunteering at the hospital. We believe that His message of love should go around, and we can’t be selfish about what we have, can we?

– The Editors

Dr. A.S. Hegde

Transcript of Dr. Hegde’s speech at SSSIHMS Whitefield on Jan 19, 2013

My humble pranaams at the Lotus Feet of Bhagawan. Sai Ram. Good Evening. It’s a great feeling to be back here. Every time I visit here which now-a-days is as frequent as once every week, it charges me with energy; I feel good. Before I say a few words, let me express my gratitude to Dr. Swarna Bharadwaj, our Director, for inviting me and my wife to the function. Thank you, Ma’am.

I can’t believe that Sri Sathya Sai Institute of Higher Medical Sciences is twelve years old. It appears like it was just the other day that we were planning the Neuro and the Cardiac facilities. Swami used to come every day to check. Those were great days, where we saw Swami’s vision and had great times with Swami.

It is sad to recall that one of our elders, who guided us through the early years, Dr. Safaya, is no more with us. I pray to Bhagawan to take him in His Fold. May his soul rest in peace.

I met Swami for the first time in 1999. It was, I think in June 1999, when He announced that the Sathya Sai Trust is going to build a hospital in Bangalore, a super-specialty hospital with Cardiac and Neuro facilities. And Swami had invited doctors from the city for a meeting one day. I was also invited for the same. That was the first time I met Swami. Later, thanks to His blessings, I was asked to plan the Neuro facility. It was my good fortune that during this planning, I met Swami many times. And I sought His blessings and visualized what was in His mind while planning this facility.

It was very interesting because the first time I asked him about the neuro facilities, He said it should be one of the best facilities in the world. The next time when I met Him, I repeated the same question. He then said, “No, we are not in competition with anybody.” I thought it was contradictory, until I realised that yes, you can be the best because you want to offer the best and not because we are in competition with any other institution. So that’s what I finally understood. It took some time for me and for a few days I was wondering whether Swami was contradicting Himself. Actually, I was the one who was confused; Swami was always very clear about what He wanted.

I must acknowledge that in parenting terms, our early parenting was very good. Swami kept coming to the hospital every now and then. He guided every activity, was there for every occasion, and with His support, we grew to be a healthy and fast-growing child. Swami inculcated the basic ethos of functioning in this Institution - it should be patient-centric, providing holistic care, care that heals? and nobody who seeks treatment will be refused. He taught us to serve without ego. And He taught that selfless service with compassion and that sacrifice is the best thing to do; so we must genuinely try to help every poor patient and heal the patient. Over a period of time, this Institution has grown from strength to strength and has come to be known for its spiritual, serene and peaceful environment. In fact, everyone who has visited the hospital over the last ten-twelve years has said that it feels different inside here, the vibrations are good and it is very peaceful. That is the usual comment people make. In real terms, Swami has turned this place into a Temple of Healing.

Swami loved people who cared for the sick, and so, doctors were His favourites. He often quoted two popular sayings, ‘Service to Man is Service to God’ and ‘Vaidyo Narayano Hari’ to inspire people to take this path of service. A solid foundation was also laid to this Institute in terms of work ethics, direction to grow and the guidelines to grow.

Swami always made it clear that the hospital is for the patients. Anytime, somebody would say, “Swami, this is your hospital” He said, “No, not My Hospital; it is your hospital.” And His principle for administration was that this hospital will treat patients first and patients first always. Attention is to the patient rather than anything else. The Institutional interest came next and the individual interest the last. Whether it is telemedicine – when we decided to have telemedicine centres at Kolkata - or any other step we took, it was the patient who mattered. Convenience of the patient was the first thing we considered.

Swami had His own ideas. I remember having met Him because we had problems with the number of nursing staff, and that too, properly trained nursing staff. Every institution in the city, state or country has different standards of training, and our standards were going up and down in terms of patient care. When I discussed this with Swami, He said, “We must have our own training programs.” That’s how the nurse training and technician training courses started. He was very clear that our country needed skilled personnel and we were going to be a part of it and we were going to be a model. And that’s what I see today that our nurses and others who have cleared the examination - the first batch - are going to be the best trained people in India in terms of practical skill, knowledge as well as in terms of their spiritual outlook and service-minded behaviour.

I remember during His last visit, Swami had directed our orientation a little bit towards research. His initial focus was clinical services to stress that aspect to us, then He emphasized skilled personnel training; and finally, He guided us to work on research. He said that we must do research but such research that is useful for our country -research regarding diseases that affect our people, research to cut costs in patient care. And He also urged us to I wish to express my grateful thanks to all those people who have worked here and who have gone out. We don’t have any regrets. I feel great pride to be a part of this family and I would like to re-affirm that if, those who have worked here inculcated even a part of this Institution’s work culture, then definitely they will serve poor patients better in their own way; and I am sure that will certainly go a long way in making this world a much better place.

A word about training. I think we should revamp the training a little bit to make it more organized while maintaining our own ethos. All of us are responsible for that and hence, we should monitor the curriculum better so that people are well-trained and do not reflect any inadequacy in their training when they work in other institutions. People who are trained here should be considered the best anywhere in the world. There should be no questions asked. And it is our responsibility to ensure that this happens.

As far as the future is concerned, I am very optimistic. We have Swami’s blessings and committed staff members and volunteers, the Seva Dal. What else can one ask for? We can’t fail. We simply cannot fail. We will succeed and reach our goal to be the number one in the world in patient care. We will continue to offer state-of-the-art technology and we will be one of the best institutions in patient care, training, and research in the world.

We should be the best patient-care and training centre in India and comparable to the best in the world. The best University. This is possible. I am sure Swami will bless us with success if we are sincere in our efforts to do that. Let us forget personal ambitions, greed and ego. Let us trust each other. Let us respect each other. Let us support each other and focus our energies to build a great institution. We owe it to Swami. This Institution should remain as the best patient-care and training centre for centuries to come. Let us pray to Swami to bless us in this endeavour.

Thank you.

Sai Ram.

Anesthesia Conference

The SSSIHMS Anesthesia Conference (SANCON) 2013 on Anesthesia Machine & Monitoring Equipment was held on 29-30 March. The focus of SANCON was to integrate textbooks with the practical use of equipment in the everyday OT Anesthesia practices. Anesthesia equipment and instrumentation is currently not covered to the extent required in postgraduate medical teaching in India. SANCON provided an opportunity to address this gap, by facilitating delegate interaction with renowned faculty using didactic sessions and workstations.

The two day conference had 18 didactic presentations and workstations by leading professionals in Anesthesia in Bangalore and across India. Over 180 medical practitioners from across India were exposed to the latest, available Anesthesia equipment.

A separate session during the conference focused on post graduate medical students facing the Diplomate of National Board (DNB) practical exams in Anesthesia. Dr. Baljit Singh, one of the leading anesthesiologists in the country, gave a number of tips to face the rigorous exam with confidence.

Dr. Pankaj Kundra of JIPMER delved on the latest techniques in Anesthesia delivery systems and touched upon how the technology evolved over the years.

Dr. Kolli Chalam, HOD of Anesthesiology, SSSIHMS-WFD; elaborated on technological advancements in equipment related to pediatric anesthetic care.

Dr. K. Muralidhar, Professor, Anesthesia, Narayana Hrudayalaya; spoke on Cardiovascular monitoring during sedation.

Dr. Thanga Prabhu, Clinical Director GE Healthcare, spoke on Anesthesia Data Management Systems and e-charting solutions for critical care patients.

Dr. Sushant Thapa GE Healthcare, provided insights on how adequacy of Anesthesia can be measured in high-risk patients through advanced monitoring equipment.

There was a separate session on Monitored Anesthesia Care, comprising five lectures focused on different monitoring techniques covering aspects related to airway, neuromuscular, neurological, and cardiovascular factors.

Considering the increasing usage of ultrasound in Anesthesia, a separate session of four lectures concentrated on how ultrasound assists in optimising patient management within the setting of Anesthesia. Ultrasound-guidance in regional Anesthesia, vascular access and hemodynamic monitoring were delved in depth by Dr. Sanjaya Viswamitra of SSSIHMS and Dr. Manimala Rao of Yashodha Hospital, Hyderabad.

The second day witnessed a rewarding panel discussion on Patent Safety & Quality, where faculty provided real world experience on tools & techniques they currently use to sustain high levels of patient safety during Anesthesia procedures.

On both days, the workshops spanned four topics: Anesthesia Machines, Monitoring, Ultrasound, eCharting and data management. GE Healthcare brought in their engineers to demonstrate the intricacies of the latest equipment in Anesthesia Monitoring and Ultrasound.

The participants pointed out that seminars on the usage of OT Anesthesia equipment are not held anywhere in Bangalore and rated the SANCON conference at par with the other two forums on this subject - RACE and ARC (held by Sri Ramachandra University and Tata Memorial Hospital respectively).

SANCON aims to be an annual forum where experts in Anesthesia can share their experiences with young medical professionals and inspire them to be responsible anesthesiologists.

Our Doctor Abroad

“I first met Prof. Juha in a micro neurosurgery workshop at the All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), New Delhi in 2011. Impressed by his surgical skills and his reputation in the area of complex cerebrovascular surgery, I wanted to spend some time with him as a clinical observer. When I approached our Director, she not only granted me a two-month leave, but she also initiated contact with the International Sai Organisation in Finland, to support me with my stay there.

Mr. Gaiyer Altay, from the Finland Sai Organization, helped me tremendously with my stay in Helsinki, and was instrumental in arranging my accommodation very close to the hospital.

The time I spent with Prof. Juha was very memorable. He is known to be one of the best cerebrovascular surgeons in the world and holds the record for clipping the largest number of intracranial aneurysms till date. Neurosurgeons from different parts of the world come to Helsinki to learn the subtle nuances of micro-neurosurgery under his able and inspiring leadership.

The two-month stint gave me an opportunity to observe complex cases of aneurysms and Arterio Venous Malformations (AVM) being operated, and to learn various new, effective techniques and apply them in daily practice.

Dr. N.A. Sai Kiran, Consultant, Neuro surgery, attended a two-month training in cerebrovascular surgery in Helsinki, Finland under Prof Juha Hernesniemi, Chairman, Department of Neurosurgery, Helsinki University Central Hospital, between 1st March to 30th April 2013.

Here’s an account of his clinical observership.

Apart from learning technical skills, it was an absolute thrill to watch the service-minded spirit of Prof. Juha. A true Karma Yogi - Prof. Juha sets a very high work ethic by inspiring young neurosurgeons with his surgical skills, dedication and hard work. To handle his load of administrative work, he is at the hospital by 4 am, even when the temperature is -200 F ! And he goes back home only by 11 pm. At times, he operates 6 to 7 cases a day.

In Finland, where education and medical care are completely free of cost, and there are no private hospitals, Prof. Juha doesn’t get any financial benefits for the hard work he puts in. It is the dedication to his profession, and the satisfaction he derives in serving society, that makes him work so hard.

He often doles out pearls of wisdom to inspire the staff working with him. He loves to say, “You must be so honest that it must hurt you.” Here are other quotable quotes:

“Work hard as hard work is difficult to beat”

“Every person owes a debt to society and has a responsibility to pay back ”

“When a student is ready, he will find his teacher”

“Surgical complications are mostly because of a lack of knowledge and surgical skills and not due to the fate of the patient”

Apart from my training under him, the two-month stay also gave me an opportunity to witness the transformation Swami’s Mission has brought about in the world. Though Finland is not a very large country, the Sai Organisation is very vibrant with each of the members being devoted to carrying out Swami’s work. They conduct bhajans every Thursday, participate in various service activities, like visiting hospitals, taking care of patients, and also in translating Swami’s literature into Finnish and Russian. Gaiyer Altay and his family are actively involved in these activities. Gaiyer Altay shared many of his experiences with Swami and how his life was transformed by Swami.

My two-month stay was an enriching experience both professionally and spiritually. Prof Juha, through his dedication to his profession and selfless service to society, reminded me of the path of Karma Yoga. Gaiyer Altay and his family, through their devotion and selfless love to Swami, reminded me of the path of Bhakthi Yoga.

Patient’s Story

The voice on the phone seemed deeply concerned. “Sister, how is Naveen?” It was a patient’s brother calling from far-away Kolkata. The patient had received physiotherapy after undergoing neurosurgery and B.S. Naveen Kumar’s counsel and support during the time had been deeply cherished. During the 12 years spent at SSSIHMS, he had offered physiotherapy to innumerable patients. In a freak incident, he was now a patient himself, and all who had heard about his condition were praying for him.

It all happened on a November night. Driving back to Bangalore in the wee hours, rushing to get back to work at the hospital, the unexpected brought his life to standstill. “I remember waking up to find that we were in a crushed car,” recalls his wife Vishalakshi. “Naveen was groaning beside me, his face disfigured and smeared with blood”. Naveen had suffered injuries to his forehead, face and jaw after the impact with the steering wheel as their car crashed into the back of a stationary lorry. The onlookers rushed the family to

a nearby government hospital, where doctors briefly evaluated their state”. As Naveen oscillated in and out of consciousness, Vishalakshi being a Neuro OT nurse,knew that he required super specialty care. In spite of other hospitals being on the way, she decided to go with her faith and chose to bring him to the Whitefield hospital. The journey took a few hours and all along the way the she had to constantly encourage Naveen to stay awake and stay strong. The moment she saw our hospital, tears welled up in her eyes. “At the hospital we were first taken to, the doctors refused to even touch the patient before filling forms. On the other hand, at our hospital, a team of doctors were waiting to rush into action, the moment Naveen was wheeled in,” says Vishalakshi with gratitude filled in her voice even today.

Faith in Swami was the only driving force and support during the next 45 days. Naveen’s injuries were serious, requiring Neurosurgical and Maxillofacial surgeries to correct the displaced bones of his face. The doctors told Vishalakshi that there might be some deficits post surgery, and he may not be able to see in one eye. But Vishalakshi knew that Swami who had brought them along this far, would not abandon them that easily.

A popular saying speaks of blessings that descend in reply to the prayers that ascend. Every evening Vishalakshi would sit before Swami’s altar at the hospital’s central dome with a single prayer. “Both my husband and I decided to serve all these years. Please allow us to continue to serve You. This is a time we need You”. The entire hospital had turned into an extended family and everyone had a prayer in their heart. Even during his recovery his optimism was infectious. When anyone would mention to him that they want him back at work, he would reply with grit and determination, “Tell them (patients) that I am coming. I am waiting to get back to work.”

Naveen is back at work today, offering instruction and counsel to patients that come to him each day for rehabilitation therapy. He can see fairly well now, but for a minor deficit in one eye. His optimism and cheerfulness continue to infect all those who work with him. “I’ve been a patient. I know how it feels,” he says each time he greets these folk with a smile. Many marvel at his recovery, saying that he is a testimony to the fact that miracles happen. He only smiles, and shares a priceless moment from the nightmare he has just emerged from. “When I was being carried away from the car after the accident, I vividly recall seeing Swami in a white robe before me,” he says. “I was in tremendous pain, and, in that state, asked Swami to save my life only if I was destined to return to work, strong and independent.” One thing is certain. When you’ve served the Lord with all your heart, He won’t let you go that easily.

Volunteering - IHS

Who said that you need to be a devotee to volunteer at the Sathya Sai hospital? All that is necessary are hearts and hands that are willing to serve. Between 15-19 April, a group of 113 young adults from IHS, a leading provider of diverse global market and economic information, located just across the Institute, offered their services to the hospital as part of their corporate social responsibility (CSR) initiative. Each individual spent one day at the Institute participating in activities that spanned work in the nutrition garden to assisting with the laundry. This initiative was taken up by the state volunteer organization, to encourage youngsters to experience service at the Institute.

Their experiences are best summarized by what they had to say as they left the institution.

Aero Show

The children on campus had a flying start to their summer holidays with the Residents’ Welfare Association organizing an aero show on 6th April during which professional aero-modellers showcased their skills at flying scaled-down models of gliders and fighter air-crafts. It was a special evening for the children, as they watched with their eyes wide open, the radio-controlled planes made of foam and plastic, soar in the sky above the lawns performing fantastic maneuvers that even the birds would envy.

A special thanks to young Sharath Malve from Botrio Design Labs Pvt. Ltd., who organized the event. It was a big hit amongst the children and adults alike. It is hoped to start an aero-modelling club on campus, where children will be taught to make chuck gliders and in time, learn to use radio-controlled planes.
