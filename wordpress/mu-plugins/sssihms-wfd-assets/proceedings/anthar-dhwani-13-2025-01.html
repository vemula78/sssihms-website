DIRECTOR’S MESSAGE - Dr D C Sundaresh

The Importance of Patient Counselling in Healthcare

Patient counselling is a cornerstone of effective healthcare delivery, serving as a critical bridge between medical professionals and patients. By fostering better understanding, adherence and trust, patient counselling empowers individuals to take an active role in their health and well-being. The key components and the impact it has on both patients and the healthcare system is reflected in the outcome of the treatment. It is a well-known fact that the patient outcome is always better when the patient is receptive to the offered treatment and has full faith in the system.

Patient Counselling at SSSIHMS, Whitefield. Patient counselling provides personalised, relevant, and comprehensible information about the proposed treatment for the given patient. The SSSIHMS is recognised as a tertiary care centre in the fields of neurosurgery, cardiology and cardiac surgery. Majority of the patients come from West Bengal, Orissa, Bihar and the neighbouring states. This poses a challenge of counselling in the language of the patient. This is effectively overcome by a team of multilingual counsellors. Here counselling goes beyond simply giving instructions; it involves building a rapport, understanding patients' concerns, and ensuring they grasp the knowledge needed to cope with the high risk surgical treatment offered to them. Counselling may include discussions about:

Potential benefits of surgery

Stress alleviating methods like prayer in their own religious methods, meditation etc. Care after surgery-importance of taking medication regularly and timely follow-up Treatment plans, prognosis, and expectations.

These methods have helped improve outcomes as documented by interviews of patients and parents. This unique approach to counselling has benefitted innumerable patients over years and is a sustainable model considering that the counsellors are all volunteers and committed to the cause. This is “SAI COUNSELLING” infused with love and gratitude.

‘TRANSFORMATION THROUGH INTROSPECTION’ - PATIENT PRASHANT'S INSPIRING STORY

Forty-five-year-old Patient Prashanth is from Mangalore district of Karnataka state. His mother tongue is Tulu. He came to our hospital, accompanied by his wife Shailaja, with a cardiac problem. He had frequent bouts of acidity and felt very tired whenever he walked and so had gone to consult a specialist in Mangalore. A CAG was done and it revealed three arterial blockages in his heart and surgery was advised. He decided to come to our Hospital because his elder brother had been successfully treated here for a heart ailment twenty years ago. Their family deity is Kali Kaamba goddess.

He was admitted on 03/09/2024 and was getting ready for the surgery when the doctor while checking the medication he was taking found he was taking a blood thinner that would be problematic for the open heart surgery. So he was asked to come after ten days; naturally he felt disappointed. The patient and his wife sounded very sad during the tele-counselling session with the counsellor. They were disappointed but decided to stay on in the hospital at the Salarpuria guest house. Noticing both the patient and his wife were unhappy about the discharge, the counsellor took them through the Body Relaxation and Mind Conditioning (BRMC) therapy which seemed to alleviate some of their disappointment and anxiety.

During the counselling session in the department, the patient mentioned that he did have a Neuro issue when he was 20 years old and was treated in his hometown Mangalore wherein a ventricular - atrial shunt surgery was performed on him.

Prashanth, a goldsmith by profession, belongs to the Vishwakarma community. He takes orders from showrooms and designs the jewellery from home. Shailaja, who accompanied him to be his caregiver, is a tailor. They have one daughter who is still in school and both parents seemed keen for her to study nursing in our Hospital.

While in the hospital, Prashanth and Shailaja would visit the counselling shrine area where they experienced a lot of solace and peace. The counselling sessions also helped them to connect with their inner spirituality and draw strength from their spiritual source Goddess Kali Kaamba.

After a month, patient was readmitted on 11/10/2022. He underwent a CABG X5 procedure on 13/10/22 and a re-exploration in view of mediastinal bleeding the next day 14/10/22. He was in the ICU for a few days. During this time a worried Shailaja reached out to the counsellor for support and felt calmer after a counselling session. Finally, after six days, on 20/10/2022, he was discharged. The discharge summary was explained to Shailaja which helped her to work on his diet, exercise and medication .

They kept in touch with the counsellor. Ten days later after returning home Prashanth developed chest congestion and Shailaja had the presence of mind to bring him immediately to the emergency department at our Hospital on 01/11/2022. The diagnosis showed right sided pleural effusion and this was corrected by placing an ICD in the right pleural space. He was kept under observation for two days after ICD removal and was discharged on 04/11/2022.

After his discharge, they both came to the Counselling Department shrine area to pray and thank Swami. Prashanth said he felt a second life was given to him in this Hospital. The counsellor observed how balanced and calm both the patient and Shailaja were considering all that they had recently been through. She felt their long stay, the hand holding by the counsellor and their own introspection process during their frequent visits to the shrine in the Counselling Department must have contributed to the transformation in them. They were very grateful to Swami, thankful to all doctors, nurses and the counsellors. They shared the second discharge summary and finally returned to Mangalore after almost three months.

They have been coming for reviews regularly and the patient is in good health. The counsellor being able to communicate with them in their mother tongue Tulu, facilitated a bond of trust and empathy with the patient and his wife whereby they could freely express their emotions without a language barrier. The patient was not just treated for his physical ailment but the continuous nurturing by the counsellor ensured he and his wife felt their mind and spirit were also restored to a sense of overall well being. In this case, because of their long stay the counsellor reaped the benefit of witnessing this transformation.

Prashant and his wife have been in touch with the counsellor since the last two years. He has resumed his work. To show their gratitude to Swami, they have been offering Seva as part of the Sevadal in our hospital for the past two years. This is their fourth trip and each time they find a lot of peace when they connect to their source in the shrine in the Counselling Department.

Swami’s Temple of Healing and the shrine within the Counselling Department therein provided solace and comfort for Prashanth and his wife Shailaja to heal completely through introspection and transformation.

“LOVE ALL, SERVE ALL” - PATIENT SUMANTA MUKHERJEE'S STORY

This is truly an extraordinary story of how Swami brought about a drastic change in the life of Patient Sumanta Mukherjee while simultaneously taking care of his chronic rheumatic heart disease over a period of seventeen years and probably many more years to come! Swami picks and chooses His instruments in His own mysterious ways but stories such as Patient Sumanta Mukherjee’s are few and far between.

In 2007 Sumanta was assailed by fatigue while walking and recurring breathlessness, chest pain and dizziness. When he consulted Burdwan Medical College in Kolkata he was advised surgery for what was evidently a heart problem. He tried consulting in different hospitals but they all said the same thing. Knowing the cost of such a surgery would be prohibitive he had lost all hope of getting better. He and his family prayed to their family deity Krishna Bhagawan. When things seemed totally hopeless for Sumanta Mukherjee, God intervened to remove the darkness; hearing about his problem, a neighbour told him about Swami’s free hospital in Bengaluru. It was as though a light had suddenly appeared to dispel the darkness.

He immediately came to SSSIHMS, accompanied by his father and a microsurgery (PTMC) was performed without delay. He was discharged with medications and asked to come back after five years for an open-heart surgery. He was also advised by our Hospital doctors to quit his current job at the cold storage since it would affect his health especially after the PTMC microsurgery. He borrowed money from friends and relatives and started a small grocery shop in his own house to meet his living expenses. In 2007 Sumantha was already married to Mum and had a young daughter. They were all happy to see his health had improved and he says he felt it was a turning point in his life. The sheer relief of being healthy again gave him a fresh insight into his potential - his leadership capability.

Having seen and experienced the way our Hospital functioned and inspired by Swami’s Vision and His quote, “Love All - Serve All”, he decided he wanted to help people. On making enquiries he found that there was a Sai Samithi in Sainthia, a nearby town. He started volunteering, selflessly serving people there. While doing so it occurred to him that he could bring the same service to his own village, Benegram/Chandrahat. He discussed the matter with the president of the Sai Samithi in Sainthia who encouraged him and under his guidance and the cooperation of people and friends from his village, a Samithi was established in 2011. Soon they had a Medical Camp twice a month, a yearly Blood Donation Camp, an Environment Awareness Program and Swachh Bharat program.

While being totally involved in this selfless endeavour, Sumanta neglected his health and exactly five years later, his original symptoms reappeared. It was probably Swami reminding him to take care of his health and as per the doctors’ orders to return for his surgery. He came for the second time to SSSIHMS in 2012 with his wife Mum as caregiver, his father having passed away.

He was admitted and MVR (Mitral Valve Replacement) surgery was recommended. By Swami’s Grace, on the day of surgery the doctors decided MVR was not required as yet and performed a second PTMC procedure. Sumanta was delighted. After surgery, filled with gratitude to Swami and the Hospital staff he returned to his hometown. He threw himself into expanding his Samithi work bestowed upon him by Swami. By this time his daughter had finished her graduation and was married. Eventually, he entrusted his shop to a reliable person and devoted his time exclusively to the services of the Samithi.

When the symptoms surfaced again twelve years later in 2024, Sumanta Mukherjee, now forty-five years old was admitted to SSSIHMS on 19/08/2024 for a MVR (Mitral Valve Replacement) surgery. He was accompanied by his wife Mum as his caregiver. This was the third time he was admitted to Swami’s hospital. When the counsellor spoke to Sumantha and his wife on the first day of his admission, she found them both calm and accepting the proposed treatment. After the counsellor took them through BRMC therapy they felt even more relaxed and were prepared for the impending surgery.

MVR surgery was done on 23/08/2024 and having recovered well he was discharged from the hospital on 30/08/2024. He was given a Discharge Summary which advised him about his medication, especially the daily intake of the prescribed dosage of Acitrom, an anticoagulant and the monthly INR check up. Fortunately he had been able to get his INR values checked at our Hospital and dosage changes, if required as advised by our doctors. The dietary department advised him of certain diet restrictions since he was prescribed Acitrom medication. The physio therapist prescribed the exercises he should follow.

Sumanta has extended their Samithi activities for cleaning the venues where periodic festivals are held in nearby well known small towns such as Shantiniketan and Kenduli, birthplace of poet Jayadeva and where the annual fair, popularly known as the Baul fair where the Bauli wandering mendicants gather, is held on Makar Sankranti.

According to Patient Sumantha, his entire life has been blessed by Baba’s Grace. He felt Baba Himself had showered His Grace and Blessings on him and his family. For the counsellor this interaction with Sumantha was an enriching experience as she noted how an individual can change the entire direction of his life towards the betterment of others rather than just taking care of himself for his family.

This is indeed a rare case of Swami dispensing His Love and Protection so that Patient Sumanta Mukherjee could fulfill his destiny.

‘DEEP GRATITUDE TO ALLAH-SAI’ - PATIENT OHIDUL RAHMAN’S STORY

Fifty-five-year-old Ohidul Rahman from Murshidabad, West Bengal, arrived at Swami’s hospital in Whitefield, Bengaluru with his son Mostafijur, as his caregiver. The patient displayed a positive attitude and a strong faith in Allah and Swami. His faith in Swami springs from a long history of his neuro ailments and resulting treatment in Swami’s hospital.

He experienced mild chest pain and sweating on 24/02/2024. They went to a local doctor and were suggested he has a CAG done. He decided to come to SSSIHMS, Whitefield, Bengaluru despite persistent leg pain which he accepted as a "test" from Allah. On 10/07/2024 after a consultation with a cardiac doctor, his blood test was done. The report showed a high level of blood sugar. After addressing the problem, he got admitted into the hospital on 19/10/2024 with his son as his caretaker. A Coronary Angiogram plus POBA, wherein a balloon is used instead of a stent, was performed successfully on 21/10/2024 and the patient was discharged on 22/10/2024. Ohidul’s son Mostafijur, who was his caretaker said that the doctors patiently explained that they decided not to put a stent because in future his Neuro problem may recur and then they will not be able to perform surgery on him. So the doctors opted for balloon surgery. Considering his past medical history as a Neuro patient in Swami’s hospital, he was advised to have a Neuro check-up. His check-up result being normal, he was given some specific exercises to practice regularly.

He was admitted to the Cardiac ward on 19/10/2024. When the counsellor connected with him on this date he mentioned how he had come to our Hospital in 2005, 2017 and 2021 for Neuro spinal surgeries before this latest admission for his heart.

Ohidul works on a commission basis at the sub-registrar's office in Murshidabad, West Bengal, where he lives with his wife, while his four children reside in Bengaluru; two are working and two are studying there. During his treatment he and his wife were staying with their daughter in Bengaluru.

Having experienced and benefitted from Swami’s hospital previously he had implicit faith and confidence in being looked after well this time as well. His wife, Sabeena Yeafmin, after seeing this also opted to get treated for her diabetes in the Outpatient department of our Hospital.

Ohidul said that in 2004 when he was just thirty-four, he developed pain in his lower back and his legs began to bend and buckle resulting in loss of his balance. There was swelling around his elbows too. He went to an Orthopaedic doctor who prescribed medication.

This health setback worried him immensely as his children were still very small and he wanted to be fit for their sakes. Fortunately, one of his cousins residing in Telangana told him about Swami’s hospital. Ohidul narrated the story of this sixty-year-old cousin who had a heart problem in 2000 and was examined and given a date for surgery in Swami’s hospital. A day prior to her surgery, when she had gone for Swami’s Darshan, Swami blessed her by touching her head. The following day when she was admitted for her procedure, the doctors told her that her heart seemed perfectly normal and she did not need any treatment or surgery. She had a calendar of SSSIHMS, Bengaluru which she had shown Ohidul.

He said his cousin’s miracle impacted him so deeply he made up his mind and came here in 2005 full of faith. He was treated for his lower back pain. A surgery for decompression of L3-L4 was performed on him and he was discharged in ten days.

He led a normal life for twelve years after that. In 2017, he was admitted here again for Canal Stenosis and discharged within a week and asked to come for a review after a year. He came a third time on 16/01/2021 when another recurrent L3 to L5 Canal Stenosis was done and he was discharged on 01/02/2021.

"The secret of perfect health lies in keeping the mind always cheerful-never worried, never hurried, never borne down by any fear, thought or anxiety."

Sathya Sai Baba

The counsellor was intrigued when Ohidul had mentioned that along with Allah, Swami too held a special place in his heart and wanted to learn more about this transformation in him. He said his cousin’s miracle had a lot to do with his first exposure to Swami. Although a devout Muslim, he is ever grateful to Sai Ram for repeatedly treating him and healing him in His hospital. He will remember this till the end of his life. He feels that everything should be surrendered to the Almighty-“Sab Allah ke marji.” He continued, “The One who gives us this life will also take care of us and nothing is in our control. Everyone should have faith and trust in the Almighty.”

The counsellor was impressed by his implicit trust in the Almighty. He follows his religion with sincerity and does “namaz” (prayers) without fail. He wishes everyone followed the edict of being nice to each other instead of finding faults in others. The counsellor then took the patient through the Body Relaxation and Mind Conditioning (BRMC) therapy, after which she prayed with him for his procedure to go smoothly on the scheduled date.

Now back at home in West Bengal, he is exercising and cycling as advised by the Neuro doctors. He cycles to work every day. He still finds it difficult to stand for long and is thankful for the help of his wife, to move about. Ohidul is overwhelmed by the kindness and compassion of the doctors and hospital staff. He expressed his heartfelt gratitude towards Swami who now has a permanent place in his heart. His family echoes his sentiments.

Thie is yet another of Swami's many miracles that have brought about life long transformation in several of our patients as has happened in the life of Patient Ohidul Rahman.

ACCEPTANCE - SAI COUNSELLING FOR CAREGIVERS - CAREGIVER SHIYULI’S STORY

One of the unique features of SAI Counselling at SSSIHMS, Whitefield is the continued counselling support provided to both patients and their caregivers admitted for neurosurgery or cardiac surgery. Why is it important to provide support to the caregiver at the hospital and thereafter? An individual’s illness involves and impacts the entire family. The greatest concern is fear and anxiety of the unknown future. Sudden illness of a serious nature affecting any family member can disrupt the normal routine in a household creating chaos. The need to adapt and adjust to new demands can take its toll on the immediate family members especially the prime caregiver. Counselling support provided to the caregivers helps them relax. The Body Relaxation and Mind Conditioning (BRMC) therapy helps them be mindful of their thoughts, feelings and faith. A shift is perceived in the caregivers as they regain lost courage and hope.

This story is about caregiver Shiyuli from Bangladesh and her journey of oscillating between hope and despair, tenacity and sorrow, perseverance and powerlessness, bravery and fearfulness, ultimately navigating with faith and courage towards prayers and surrender to their God, Allah, culminating in acceptance of the inevitable.

Shiyuli and her husband Abdul Latif (a welder by profession working in Singapore) are from Bangladesh and belong to a loving close knit family. The family rejoiced when they were blessed with a son after two daughters sixteen-year-old Sumaiya and eight-year-old Sadhiya. They named their son Mohammed Abu Said. Unfortunately the celebration and their happiness was short lived when Abu had symptoms of breathlessness and unease. Physicians at the local medical centres failed to diagnose his condition and they were advised to consult doctors in India at SSSIHMS, Whitefield.

In the year 2023 one-year-old Abu had a thorough investigation at SSSIHMS Whitefield and was diagnosed with a genetic condition called Noonan syndrome, that stops typical development in various parts of the body. This illness can affect a person in several ways, many individuals with Noonan syndrome are born with heart problems (congenital heart disease) that causes some of the key symptoms of the condition. Abu was born with congenital heart problems which had to be treated. Doctors manage Noonan syndrome by controlling the symptoms and complications since there is no cure for this illness. It has to be endured.

Shiyuli and Abdul Latiff travelled with Abu from Bangladesh to India SSSIHMS in 2023. Abu needed immediate attention and medical aid and they gave their consent for the cardiac procedure suggested by the doctors. Abu underwent a PVBD (Pulmonary Valve Balloon Dilatation) and was discharged with the advice for a follow up in 2024.

Reflecting upon her first visit to SSSIHMS Shiyuli recalls how desperation was replaced by hope and joy when their child received loving care at SSSIHMS and was discharged after a successful cardiac procedure in 2023.

The second visit to India involved a great amount of planning and expenditure for the family. They made arrangements for their two daughters Sumaiya and Sadhiya to stay with relatives in Bangladesh. Abdul Latiff had to apply for leave and his visa had to be organised to enable him to travel from Singapore to Bangladesh and later to India. Shiyuli’s brother offered to accompany them to India and support Shiyuli since Abdul Latiff had limited leave sanctioned from work. Illness is a family affair where the caregivers have to adjust and comply with various changes and demands.

On 13/07/2024 Mohammed Abu Said was admitted at SSSIHMS for cardiac surgery and the CTVS team performed the following successful cardiac surgeries - ASD patch closure, Pulmonary Valvotomy, MPA augmentation plasty, RVOT muscle bundle resection and PDA ligation. Abu’s symptoms caused by Noonan syndrome had to be controlled through the above surgeries. Since Latiff Abu’s father had exhausted his leave he had to return to Singapore to resume his duty. Shiyuli shouldered the responsibility of being the prime caregiver to Abu and was grateful to her brother for his support. She waited patiently in the corridor outside ICU for weeks together where her tenacity and love for her child helped her endure many challenges such as loneliness, homesickness and concern about her two young daughters and her home in Bangladesh. The long wait outside ICU for more than a month took its toll on Shiyuli.

Dr Lahari a compassionate and empathetic doctor at SSSIHMS who had interacted and evaluated Abu’s condition in cardiac OPD before and after his admission and thereafter observed Shiyuli’s anxiety, stress and emotional pain and referred her for counselling. This initiative helped provide continued counselling support to Shiyuli the caregiver from 17/08/24 to 24/08/24. On day one, 17/08/24, listening to the voice of the counsellor Shiyuli broke down in tears sobbing uncontrollably. Absorbing in silence the pain and suffering of the young mother and providing her the space to express her concerns between sobs, the counsellor provided an opportunity for Shiyuli to unburden her feelings uninhibitedly. There was a slight shift in the frequency of her sobs and this seemed an opportune moment for the counsellor to intervene and introduce the Adhan/call for prayers... She was able to tune into this field of spiritual energy when she heard the familiar call of the Adhan and fervently connected to her spiritual dimension by offering prayers to Allah.

The next session on 18/08/2024 she met the counsellor in the department and was happy to receive a copy of the Holy Koran. She practiced the BRMC therapy in the department which provided her with solace. Six days of continued support brought about a gentle yet definite shift within her.
